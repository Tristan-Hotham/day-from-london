## Summary of the contribution

How much of Great Britain lay within a day of London, and how did that change from the coaching era to today? This paper builds one transparent network model on a 2 km grid for five dates, using the Cambridge Group's historical GIS of turnpike roads and of the 1851 railways, a reconstruction of the 1910 network and OpenStreetMap. Coach speeds are fitted to 136 timetabled London services of 1836 and to cited journeys of the 1740s to 1760s; train speeds come from Leunig's published averages; every era is validated on journeys it did not see. Twelve hours from Charing Cross reached 5% of the land in 1750 (a rough sketch: the 1750 model misses its documented journeys by more than the limit set in advance), 19% in the 1830s, at least 47% in 1850, at least 72% in 1910 (lower bounds, since the trains are average trains) and 97% today. The paper turns point-to-point histories of time-space convergence into tested national surfaces and releases them for reuse.
